**[Fit for duty](/glossary#fit-for-duty)** is defined in
[CARs 101.01](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/section-101.01.html):
your ability to act as a flight crew member _"is not impaired by fatigue, the
consumption of alcohol or drugs or any mental or physical condition."_ Only the
flight crew member can decide whether they are fatigued to that extent (AC 700-047
§ 4.2).

## Before an FDP

If, **before** the FDP begins, you tell the operator you're fatigued to the point
of not being fit for duty, the operator **must not** let you start it
(700.26(1)). If you realise it before reporting, tell the operator as soon as you
can (AC 700-047 § 4.11).

## During an FDP

- **Two or more crew:** tell every other flight crew member and the operator as
  soon as you're aware you've become unfit (700.26(2)). On the ground, you'd be
  removed from flight duty; airborne, the other pilot manages the flight to a safe
  landing (AC 700-047 § 4.12).
- **Single pilot:** advise the operator immediately, or — if airborne — as soon
  as possible after landing (700.26(3); AC 700-047 § 4.13).

## Reporting limit breaches and missed rest

- If you (or anyone) become aware that an assignment would exceed the maximum
  flight time, FDP or hours of work, tell the operator as soon as possible
  (700.26(4)).
- If a required rest period or time free from duty wasn't granted, tell the
  operator as soon as possible (700.26(5)).

## Unfit for the assigned duty but fit for a shorter one?

The CARs don't address partial fitness. See
[Unfit for one duty but fit for another](/qa/unfit).
