Section 700.41 is about a **duty pattern**: when your hours of work switch
**between early duty and late or night duty** — a run of early starts followed by
a late finish or a night flight, or the other way round. When that happens, you
need extra recovery on top of the normal [rest period](/rules/700-40).

> **On the term.** "Disruptive schedule" is **not defined** in the regulations —
> it is the heading of section 700.41 ("Rest Period — Disruptive Schedules"), and
> [headings are not part of the enactment](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/i-21/section-14.html).
> The three duty types it turns on **are** defined, in
> [CARs 700.01](/glossary):
>
> - **early duty** — hours of work that **begin between 02:00 and 06:59**;
> - **late duty** — hours of work that **end between midnight and 01:59**;
> - **night duty** — hours of work that **begin between 13:00 and 01:59 and end
>   after 01:59**.
>
> All three are measured at the location where you are acclimatized.

## What's required

When a flight crew member goes **from late duty or night duty to the following
early duty**, or **from early duty to the following late duty or night duty**, the
air operator must provide — **in addition to** the [700.40](/rules/700-40) rest
period — **one local night's rest** (700.41(1); AC 700-047 § 4.41).

A **[local night's rest](/glossary#local-nights-rest)** is a rest period of at
least nine hours that takes place between **22:30 and 09:30** at the location
where the flight crew member is acclimatized (CARs 700.01).

For this purpose "duty" means any activity that meets the definition of duty, and
positioning that is not immediately followed by a flight duty period is treated as
a flight duty period (700.43(4); AC 700-047 § 4.41).

## When it doesn't apply

Section 700.41 does **not** apply when you are at a location more than **four
hours** off the local time where you were last acclimatized (700.41(2); AC
700-047 § 4.42). In that situation the [time-zone rest rules in 700.42](/rules/700-42)
take over.
