Section 700.72 permits a short, planned rest taken **on the flight deck during a
flight**, as a countermeasure for unexpected in-flight fatigue. AC 700-047 § 4.91
describes it as _"an opportunity for a flight crew member to obtain rest during a
flight."_ It is not a defined term, and it is not something you can plan around —
you must be **[fit for duty](/glossary#fit-for-duty)** when you report, because
there is no guarantee controlled rest will be possible. The conditions in
700.72(1)–(4) are what actually govern it.

## When it's allowed

All of the following must hold (700.72(1)):

- the rest is **45 minutes or less**,
- it is taken **during cruise**,
- it ends **at least 30 minutes before** the scheduled start of descent,
- **no other flight crew member** is resting at the same time, and
- **at least two flight crew members** remain on the flight deck.

## Before the rest

The resting crew member must (700.72(2)):

- **hand over** their duties to a crew member who is not resting,
- review the **status of the flight** and any duties to be done during the rest,
- review the **wake-up criteria**, and
- tell the **flight attendants** the start and end times.

## After the rest

- No duties for **15 minutes** after the rest ends — a recovery buffer for sleep
  inertia (700.72(3)).
- Another crew member gives an **operational briefing** on return to duty
  (700.72(4)).
