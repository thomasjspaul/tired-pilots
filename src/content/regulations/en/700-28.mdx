The **flight duty period** (FDP) is defined in
[CARs 101.01](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/section-101.01.html).
It **begins at the earliest** of: carrying out any assigned duties before
reporting for a flight; reporting for a flight (the first flight, if there is more
than one); reporting for positioning; or reporting as a flight crew member on
standby. It **ends at engines off or rotors stopped** at the end of a flight.
Section 700.28 sets the maximum FDP an air operator may schedule and a flight crew
member may accept. See the [glossary](/glossary) for the other defined terms used
on this page.

## The basic idea

There is no single number. Your maximum FDP is read from a table, and three
things choose the row and column:

- **When the FDP starts** — the local time you report, at the place you are
  acclimatized to. Earlier and overnight starts get shorter limits.
- **How many flights** are scheduled in the FDP.
- **The average length of those flights** — under 30 minutes, 30 to under 50
  minutes, or 50 minutes or more. Shorter average flights mean more take-offs
  and landings, so the limits are tighter.

Flights conducted under **day VFR** use their own, simpler table with no
flight-count column.

## How to read the tables

1. **Pick the table** for your average flight duration (or the day-VFR table).
2. **Find the row** for the time your FDP starts.
3. **Find the column** for the number of flights scheduled.
4. The cell is your maximum FDP.

**Average flight duration** is the total scheduled flight time divided by the
number of flights. **Positioning** (dead-head) legs do not count as flights for
choosing the column, but positioning time still counts as duty.

## Acclimatization

The tables above are read using a start time that is **the local time at the
place you are acclimatized to** — not necessarily the clock on the wall where the
FDP begins (700.28(5); [AC 700-047](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/reference-centre/advisory-circulars/advisory-circular-ac-no-700-047)
§ 4.22–4.26).

- **If you are acclimatized** to the place the FDP begins, the start time is
  simply local time. Use that row.
- **If you are not acclimatized** to that place, convert your report time to the
  local time at the zone you are still acclimatized to, and use _that_ row.

Because the home‑zone equivalent is often earlier in the day, the maximum FDP can
drop sharply.

> **Worked examples (from AC 700-047 § 4.22).**
> Acclimatized to Calgary (Mountain), you report in Toronto (Eastern) at 07:00
> local. Toronto is two zones east, so your start time for the table is
> **05:00** — use the 05:00–05:59 row, not 07:00–12:59.
> Acclimatized to Halifax (Atlantic), you report in Vancouver (Pacific) at 07:00
> local. Your start time for the table is **11:00** — still the 07:00–12:59 row.

### When you count as acclimatized

An operator may assume you are acclimatized to your **home‑base** time zone
unless previous _duty_ has acclimatized you somewhere else. Only operating or
positioning to a new zone — and then spending enough time there — changes it; a
there‑and‑back trip the next day does not (AC 700-047 § 4.23).

After leaving the zone you are acclimatized to, you become acclimatized to the
new location once required rest has been provided **and** you have spent:

**also applies to the day‑VFR table** in subsection (9).

## Positioning, standby and time zones

- **Positioning** after you report counts as flight duty time, but a positioning
  leg is **not** counted as one of the "flights" for choosing the table column
  (700.28(6)).
- A **standby** flight duty period begins when you report for duty at the
  location the operator designates (700.28(8)).
- The Canadian time zones for this purpose are Pacific, Mountain, Central,
  Eastern, and Atlantic (which includes Newfoundland and Labrador) (700.28(7)).

The verbatim wording of all nine subsections is in **Regulation text — CARs
700.28** below.

## When this applies

Section 700.28 has applied to **705** (airline) operations since **12 December
2020** and to **703 / 704** (air taxi and commuter) operations since **12
December 2022**. Until the 703/704 date, those operators were allowed to keep
complying with the former flight-time and duty rules.
