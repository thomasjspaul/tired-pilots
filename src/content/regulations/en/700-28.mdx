The **flight duty period** (FDP) is the time from when you report for a flight (or
series of flights) until the last flight ends. Section 700.28 sets the maximum
FDP an air operator may schedule and a flight crew member may accept.

## The basic idea

There is no single number. Your maximum FDP is read from a table, and three
things choose the row and column:

- **When the FDP starts** — the local time you report, at the place you are
  acclimatized to. Earlier and overnight starts get shorter limits.
- **How many flights** are scheduled in the FDP.
- **The average length of those flights** — under 30 minutes, 30 to under 50
  minutes, or 50 minutes or more. Shorter average flights mean more take-offs
  and landings, so the limits are tighter.

Flights conducted under **day VFR** use their own, simpler table with no
flight-count column.

## How to read the tables

1. **Pick the table** for your average flight duration (or the day-VFR table).
2. **Find the row** for the time your FDP starts.
3. **Find the column** for the number of flights scheduled.
4. The cell is your maximum FDP.

**Average flight duration** is the total scheduled flight time divided by the
number of flights. **Positioning** (dead-head) legs do not count as flights for
choosing the column, but positioning time still counts as duty.

## Acclimatization

The tables above assume you are **acclimatized** — your body clock is aligned
with local time where the FDP starts.

Under section 700.28, you are generally considered acclimatized when you have had
the required rest and have spent enough time in the same time zone: about **72
hours** for a time-zone difference of less than 4 hours, or **96 hours** for a
difference of 4 hours or more — or, put another way, roughly **24 hours for each
hour** of time-zone difference from the place you were last acclimatized.

If you are **not** acclimatized, you look up the table using the FDP start time
**expressed in the time zone you are still acclimatized to**, not the local clock
where you happen to be. Because that home-zone time is often in the overnight or
early-morning band, the maximum FDP can drop sharply.

> **Check this one carefully.** The exact acclimatization mechanic — and how it
> interacts with the tables — is the part of 700.28 most easily misread. Confirm
> against [Advisory Circular 700-047](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/reference-centre/advisory-circulars/advisory-circular-ac-no-700-047)
> and your company Operations Manual before relying on a number.

## Positioning, standby and time zones

- **Positioning** after reporting counts as flight duty time, but a positioning
  leg is not counted as one of the "flights" for the table column.
- A **standby** duty period starts when you report to the location the operator
  assigns.
- Canada is divided into time zones for the purpose of deciding the reference
  time for acclimatization.

## When this applies

Section 700.28 has applied to **705** (airline) operations since **12 December
2020** and to **703 / 704** (air taxi and commuter) operations since **12
December 2022**. Until the 703/704 date, those operators were allowed to keep
complying with the former flight-time and duty rules.
