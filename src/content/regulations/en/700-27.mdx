**Flight time** is defined in [CARs 101.01](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/section-101.01.html) as _"the time from the moment an aircraft first moves under its own power for the purpose of taking off until the moment it comes to rest at the end of the flight."_

That is **block time** (chocks-off to chocks-on): it **includes taxi time**. It is **not** the same as **air time**, which CARs 101.01 defines for technical records as the time _"from the moment an aircraft leaves the surface until it comes into contact with the surface at the next point of landing"_ (wheels-up to wheels-down). Section 700.27 caps your **flight time**.

The single-pilot limit covers flights under **both** VFR and IFR (AC 700-047 § 4.16).

## How the rolling limits work

Each limit is a running total over the window ending **now**. To find how much you can fly today, add up your flight time over the previous 27, 89 and 364 days (not counting today) and subtract each from the matching cap (AC 700-047 § 4.16(4)):

- Flown 103.4 hours in the previous 27 days → 112 − 103.4 = **8.6 hours** available today.
- Flown 290.2 hours in the previous 89 days → 300 − 290.2 = **9.8 hours** available today.

Your available time today is the **smallest** of the four results.

## What counts

- **All of your flight time**, including flying for another air operator, a private operator, and any private or military flying. You must tell your operator about it, and the operator must include it (700.27(2)(a); AC 700-047 § 4.17, read with 700.20).
- On an **augmented crew** flight, the **whole flight's** flight time counts for every flight crew member — not just the time you spend at the controls (700.27(2)(b); AC 700-047 § 4.18).
