An **[unforeseen operational circumstance](/glossary#unforeseen-operational-circumstance)** (UOC) is defined in [CARs 101.01](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/section-101.01.html) as _"an event, such as unforecast adverse weather, or an equipment malfunction or air traffic control delay, that is beyond the control of an air operator or private operator."_ To be usable here it must arise **within 60 minutes of the start of**, or **during**, the flight duty period (AC 700-047 § 4.70).

Something known **more than 60 minutes** before you report is _not_ unforeseen and cannot be used to extend the FDP. Delays after the FDP ends (customs, inspections, airport issues) are recorded as duty time, not UOC.

## What the PIC may do

If the pilot-in-command judges that a UOC could cause fatigue that may affect the safety of the flight, then **after consulting all crew members** on their fatigue, the PIC may (700.63(1)):

- **reduce** a flight crew member's flight duty period;
- **extend** the FDP beyond the [700.28](/rules/700-28) or [700.60](/rules/700-60) maximum by:

- or **extend** the rest period.

The authority is the **PIC's alone**. The operator may set _more_ restrictive guidance but cannot make the decision (AC 700-047 § 4.71).

## After the extension

- If a further UOC arises **after take-off on the final flight**, the PIC may continue to the destination or an alternate (700.63(2)).
- The operator must extend the following rest period by **at least the amount of the FDP extension** (700.63(3); AC 700-047 § 4.73). Extend the FDP by 1.5 hours → the next rest period grows by 1.5 hours.
- At the end of the FDP the PIC must **notify the operator** of any change made under this section (700.63(4)).
