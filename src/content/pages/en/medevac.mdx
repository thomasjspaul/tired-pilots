> **Secondary reference.** Medical evacuation flights run on **Division IV of Subpart 700** (sections 700.100–700.135), **not** the [703/704/705 fatigue rules](/rules) — there are no 700.28 start-time tables and no acclimatization mechanic. This page paraphrases every section in the Division; the [authoritative text is on Justice Laws](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/section-700.100.html).

## Who this Division covers — 700.100

The Division applies to an air operator that conducts a **medical evacuation flight** and to the flight crew members who fly it (700.100(1)). It does **not** apply to operations under [Subpart 702](/702-aerial-work) — aerial work (700.100(2)).

Positioning an aircraft **before or after** a medevac flight counts as a medevac flight (700.100(3)). A flight dispatched primarily to carry cargo is **not** a medevac flight even if it happens to carry human blood products, organs or medical supplies (AC 700-047).

_"Medical evacuation flight" is defined in [CARs 101.01](/glossary#medical-evacuation-flight)._

## Monitoring system and records — 700.101

The air operator must have a system that monitors each flight crew member's **flight time, flight duty periods and rest periods**, and must set out how it works in the company operations manual (700.101(1)). If a crew member — or anyone else — realises an assignment would blow past the 700.103 flight-time limit or the 700.104 maximum FDP, they must tell the operator **as soon as possible** (700.101(2)).

Where the operator also uses these crew members on non-medevac flying, **all** duty has to be accounted for, not just the medevac hours (AC 700-047).

## Fitness for duty — 700.102

- Before an FDP starts, if the crew member tells the operator they are **fatigued to the point of not being fit for duty**, the operator must not let them begin (700.102(1)).
- If fatigue reaches that point **during** an FDP, the crew member must tell every other crew member and the operator as soon as they become aware of it (700.102(2)).
- In a **single-pilot** aircraft, the pilot advises the operator immediately or, if airborne, as soon as possible after landing (700.102(3)).
- Anyone who becomes aware that an assignment would exceed the maximum flight time or maximum FDP must advise the operator as soon as possible (700.102(4)).
- If a rest period or time free from duty was **not** provided, the crew member or anyone else who knows must advise the operator as soon as possible (700.102(5)).

## Flight time — 700.103

An operator must not assign, and a crew member must not accept, flight time that would push the member's **total flight time** over any of these limits (700.103(1)):

| Limit       | Window                    | Applies to                                                              |
| ----------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 40 hours    | any 7 consecutive days    | 704 / 705 flights in an aircraft **other than a helicopter**           |
| 60 hours    | any 7 consecutive days    | 703 flights, or **any helicopter**                                     |
| 120 hours   | any 30 consecutive days   | all — **100 hours** if the member is **on call**                       |
| 300 hours   | any 90 consecutive days   | all                                                                    |
| 1,200 hours | any 365 consecutive days  | all                                                                    |
| 8 hours     | any 24 consecutive hours  | **single-pilot** operations only                                       |

Total flight time here **includes** flight time from other flying, and the **whole** duration of a flight flown with an augmented crew (700.103(2)).

## Flight duty period — 700.104

A flat maximum of **14 hours** (700.104). There are no start-time tables — the 14-hour ceiling applies regardless of when the FDP begins.

_Sections 700.105–700.115 are reserved (they do not exist)._

## Rest period — 700.116

At the end of an FDP the operator must provide a rest period of **10 hours plus the travel time** to and from the place the rest is taken (700.116(1)). The operator must give **advance notice** of the rest period and its length (700.116(2)).

## Rest period — positioning — 700.117

If the operator requires travel for **positioning after** an FDP, it must add a rest period of **at least half of the travel time that exceeds the member's maximum FDP** (700.117).

## Split flight duty — 700.118

If an FDP contains a rest period, the operator may extend it past the 700.104 14-hour maximum by **half the length of that rest period, up to 3 hours**, if (700.118(1)):

- the operator gives notice of the extension **before** the FDP begins;
- the rest period is **at least 4 consecutive hours in suitable accommodation**; and
- the operator does not interrupt it.

The rest period **after** that FDP is then extended by at least the amount of the extension (700.118(2)).

## Time free from duty — 700.119

The operator must provide **one** of the following (700.119(1)):

- **≥ 36 consecutive hours** within each 7-day period;
- **≥ 3 consecutive days** within each 17-day period; or
- **≥ 4 consecutive days** within each 19-day period.

Advance notice of the time free from duty and its length is required (700.119(2)).

## Consecutive flight duty periods — 700.120

After **three consecutive FDP assignments that each exceed 12 consecutive hours**, the operator must give **≥ 24 consecutive hours free from flight duty** — unless the member already had a 24-consecutive-hour rest **between each** of those FDPs (700.120).

## Delayed reporting time — 700.121

If the operator tells the crew member — **before they leave the rest facility** — of a delay of **more than 3 hours** to the reporting time, the FDP is treated as starting **3 hours after** the original reporting time (700.121).

_Sections 700.122–700.130 are reserved (they do not exist)._

## Augmented crew and rest facilities — 700.131

If the operator adds **one** flight crew member and provides a rest facility, the maximum FDP becomes (700.131(1)):

| Maximum FDP | Rest facility               |
| ----------- | --------------------------- |
| 15 hours    | a flight deck observer seat |
| 17 hours    | class 2                     |
| 20 hours    | class 1                     |

This applies only to an FDP with **three flights or fewer** (700.131(2)). **All** time spent in the rest facility counts as FDP (700.131(3)). If the FDP was extended this way, the following rest period **equals the duration of the duty period just completed** (700.131(4)).

## Long-range flights — 700.132

An FDP with a flight — or series of flights — that ends **more than four one-hour time zones** from the point of departure (unless flown entirely within Northern Domestic Airspace) is limited to **three flights** and must be followed by a rest period **at least equal to the FDP** (700.132(1)).

If that flight is **transoceanic**, only **one** flight may be flown after it, not counting one unscheduled technical stop (700.132(2)).

## Unforeseen operational circumstances — 700.133

As a result of an unforeseen operational circumstance, and **after consulting all crew members on their level of fatigue**, the **pilot-in-command** may extend a crew member's flight time and FDP beyond the 700.103 and 700.104 maximums (700.133(1)).

The extension may be **up to 3 hours** if the PIC notifies the operator of its length and reason; where an FDP is extended, the following rest period is increased by **at least the length of the extension** (700.133(2)). The operator keeps a record of every such notification for **24 months** (700.133(3)).

_(This is a single flat 3-hour allowance — unlike the tiered [700.63](/rules/700-63) extensions in the 703/704/705 rules.)_

## Flight crew member on reserve — 700.134

The operator must give each crew member **on reserve** a rest period of **at least 10 consecutive hours in any 24 consecutive hours** if it (700.134(1)):

- gives **24 hours' notice** of the rest period's start and length;
- gives **at least 10 hours' notice** and assigns no duty during those 10 hours; or
- assigns no FDP and does not interrupt the rest between **22:00 and 06:00** local time.

Once that rest is given, the start of the next rest period cannot move by more than **3 hours** from the previous start time, or by more than **8 hours total in any 7 consecutive days** (700.134(2)).

If the operator **cannot** provide that rest and the member is called for duty — or the reporting time falls between **22:00 and 06:00** local — then the maximum FDP is **10 hours** and the following rest period is increased by **at least half the length of the preceding FDP** (700.134(3)).

## Controlled rest on the flight deck — 700.135

A flight crew member may take controlled rest on the flight deck only if (700.135(1)):

- it is **45 minutes or less**, taken in the **cruise** portion, and finished **at least 30 minutes before** the scheduled start of descent;
- **no other** crew member is resting at the same time; and
- **at least two** flight crew members stay on the flight deck.

Before resting, the member hands over their duties, reviews the status of the flight and the wake-up criteria, and tells the other crew members the start and end times (700.135(2)). They take on **no duties until 15 minutes after** the rest ends (700.135(3)), and get an **operational briefing** when they return to duty (700.135(4)).

_Sections 700.136–700.199 are reserved (they do not exist)._
