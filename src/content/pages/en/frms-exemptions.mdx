> **Secondary reference.** This is an operator-level regime, summarised here so
> you know what it means when a schedule runs on an FRMS exemption. The
> authoritative sources are
> [CARs 700.200–700.259](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/section-700.200.html),
> [AC 700-046 — FRMS Requirements](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/reference-centre/advisory-circulars/advisory-circular-ac-no-700-046),
> and
> [AC 700-045 — Exemption and Safety Case Process](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/reference-centre/advisory-circulars/advisory-circular-ac-no-700-045).

## What it is

The prescriptive rules — the [700.28 tables](/rules/700-28), rest periods,
reserve limits and so on — are a one-size-fits-all safety net. An **FRMS** lets
an operator depart from specific prescriptive limits **for a defined flight**, if
it can show, with data and science, that the way that flight is actually run is
**no more fatiguing** than the prescriptive limit would be.

"Flight" here also means a **series of flights** flown by the same crew in one or
consecutive flight duty periods (CARs 700.201).

## How an operator gets there (700.200, 700.206)

1. **Establish and implement** the two core FRMS components — the **fatigue risk
   management plan** and the **fatigue risk management process** — plus, over
   time, an FRMS **promotion** program and a **quality assurance** program.
2. **Send a Notice of Intent** to the Minister (700.206) stating that the plan
   and process are in place, describing the flight, listing the exact provisions
   to be departed from, describing the **variance**, and citing the **scientific
   studies** showing the variance won't adversely affect crew fatigue and
   alertness.
3. On sending a compliant Notice of Intent, the **initial exemption** applies —
   the operator can begin flying the flight under the exemption.
4. Keep the required **fatigue analysis** available to the Minister for review
   every 90 days (or at the end of a shorter period).

## Staying exempt (the safety case and audits)

The **initial exemption** is time-limited. To keep flying under it the operator
must build and submit a **safety case** — the evidence that the variance is being
managed — pass an **initial audit**, and then run **continuing audits** and
**variance monitoring** for as long as the exemption is used. If the operator
can't keep the FRMS running to standard, the exemption stops and the flight
reverts to the prescriptive limits.

## What it means for a crew member

- Your operator can only depart from the **specific provisions named in its
  Notice of Intent**, and only for the **specific flight** it applies to —
  everything else still runs on the prescriptive rules.
- The exemption rests on the operator collecting and acting on **fatigue data**,
  including crew reports. An internal fatigue reporting route is part of the FRMS.
- You still have the [700.26](/rules/700-26) duty to declare when you're not fit
  for duty, and the FRMS doesn't change that.
