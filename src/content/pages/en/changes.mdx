This site focuses on the rules **as they are now**. This page is the short
history of how they got here.

## What happened

After roughly a decade of work to bring Canada's pilot duty and rest rules in
line with modern fatigue science, the final rule was published on **12 December
2018** in the _Canada Gazette_, Part II, as **SOR/2018-269**. It amended the
Canadian Aviation Regulations (SOR/96-433).

The old regime was built mainly on **flight-time limits** plus a fixed maximum
duty day (14 hours, extendable to 17 with rest). The new regime replaces that
with:

- **science-based flight-duty-period tables** ([700.28](/rules/700-28)) keyed to
  the time of day the duty starts, the number of flights, and their average
  length;
- explicit ceilings on **hours of work** ([700.29](/rules/700-29));
- rules for **disruptive schedules** ([700.41](/rules/700-41)), **time-zone
  rest** ([700.42](/rules/700-42)), **reserve and standby**
  ([700.70](/rules/700-70)–[700.71](/rules/700-71)), **controlled rest on the
  flight deck** ([700.72](/rules/700-72)), and a **Fatigue Risk Management
  System** route for operators that can show a schedule is not fatiguing;
- a shift from **bottle-to-throttle 8 hours to 12 hours** for alcohol (CARs
  602.03).

## The old-versus-new detail

The previous version of this site walked through the changes section by section,
with the old and new text side by side. That material is being folded into the
individual rule pages as a **"What changed in 2018"** expander, rather than kept
as a separate archive. For the authoritative before/after, the amending
instrument is
[SOR/2018-269 in the Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2018/2018-12-12/html/sor-dors269-eng.html),
and the pre-amendment text is available as a
[dated version on the Justice Laws website](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/).
