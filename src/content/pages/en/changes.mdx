This site focuses on the rules **as they are now**. This page is the short history of how they got here.

## What happened

After roughly a decade of work to bring Canada's pilot duty and rest rules in line with modern fatigue science, the final rule was published on **12 December 2018** in the _Canada Gazette_, Part II, as **SOR/2018-269**. It amended the Canadian Aviation Regulations (SOR/96-433).

The old regime was built mainly on **flight-time limits** plus a fixed maximum duty day (14 hours, extendable to 17 with rest). The new regime replaces that with:

- **science-based flight-duty-period tables** ([700.28](/rules/700-28)) keyed to the time of day the duty starts, the number of flights, and their average length;
- explicit ceilings on **hours of work** ([700.29](/rules/700-29));
- rules for **disruptive schedules** ([700.41](/rules/700-41)), **time-zone rest** ([700.42](/rules/700-42)), **reserve and standby** ([700.70](/rules/700-70)–[700.71](/rules/700-71)), **controlled rest on the flight deck** ([700.72](/rules/700-72)), and a **Fatigue Risk Management System** route for operators that can show a schedule is not fatiguing;
- a shift from **bottle-to-throttle 8 hours to 12 hours** for alcohol (CARs 602.03).

## Related Part I and Part VI changes

SOR/2018-269 also amended rules outside Subpart 700. The ones worth knowing:

- **New defined terms — [CARs 101.01](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/section-101.01.html).** The amendment added the Part I definitions the fatigue rules are built on — _flight duty period_, _fit for duty_, _rest period_, _positioning_, _home base_, _suitable accommodation_ and _unforeseen operational circumstance_. They are reproduced in the [glossary](/glossary).
- **Fitness for duty — [CARs 602.02](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/section-602.02.html).** An operator must not require a person to act as a flight crew member, and a person must not act as one, if either has reason to believe the person is not — or is not likely to be — **fit for duty**. It sits in the general operating rules, so it covers every operation, not just 703/704/705, and it backs up the specific declaration duty in [700.26](/rules/700-26).
- **Suitable accommodation away from home base — [CARs 602.47](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/section-602.47.html).** The operator must provide **suitable accommodation** for rest periods taken away from home base. The Subpart 700 rest and time-zone rules assume that standard is met.
- **Alcohol — CARs 602.03.** The bottle-to-throttle window went from 8 hours to 12 hours (noted above).
- **Private and corporate operators — Subpart 604.** Business and corporate aviation running under [Subpart 604](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/section-604.01.html) has its **own** fatigue provisions in Division III of that subpart. Those are not the 703/704/705 rules and are not covered on this site.
