**Acclimatized** is defined in [CARs 700.01](/glossary#acclimatized) as describing
a flight crew member _"whose biorhythm is aligned with local time."_ In practical
terms, your body clock is aligned with the local day and night where you're
acclimatized.

## Why it matters

The [700.28](/rules/700-28) maximum-FDP tables use the start time of your flight
duty period **at the location where you're acclimatized**. That may not be the
same as the local time where your FDP actually begins (AC 700-047 § 4.19(5)).

- **Acclimatized** to where the FDP begins → use the local start time.
- **Not acclimatized** → convert the start time to the local time at the location
  where you're acclimatized, and use that time in the FDP table.

That conversion can move your start time into a different row of the FDP table and
therefore change your maximum FDP. For example, a 07:00 report in Toronto while
still acclimatized to Calgary is treated as a 05:00 start for the purposes of the
table.

## When you count as acclimatized

An air operator may assume you're acclimatized to your **home-base** time zone
unless previous duty has acclimatized you to a different time zone. Only duty can
acclimatize you to another time zone — for example, operating or positioning to a
new time zone and remaining there long enough to become acclimatized. A trip to
another time zone followed by a return to your starting location the next day does
not normally change where you're acclimatized (AC 700-047 § 4.23).

CARs 700.28(5) provides three ways to determine acclimatization:

- **Less than 4 hours' difference:** you're considered acclimatized after the
  required rest periods have been provided and you've spent **72 hours** in the
  same time zone.
- **4 hours' difference or more:** you're considered acclimatized after the
  required rest periods have been provided and you've spent **96 hours** in the
  same time zone.
- **Daily adjustment:** you may adjust your acclimatized time by **1 hour toward
  the new time zone for every 24 hours** spent there.

That last method means your acclimatized time can progressively move toward the
new time zone rather than remaining fixed at your original time zone until the 72-
or 96-hour threshold is reached (CARs 700.28(5); AC 700-047 §§ 4.23–4.26).

For the purposes of these rules, CARs 700.28(7) treats the Canadian time zones as
**Pacific, Mountain, Central, Eastern and Atlantic**, with the Atlantic time zone
including Newfoundland and Labrador.

## A note on other operations

The **medical evacuation flight** rules in the CARs 700.100 series use their own
flight-time, FDP and rest provisions. Operations under **Subpart 702 (Aerial
Work)** are also subject to different requirements. If either applies to your
operation, check the applicable provisions directly rather than assuming the
700.28 acclimatization rules apply.
