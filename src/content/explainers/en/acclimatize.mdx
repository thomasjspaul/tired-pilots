**Acclimatized** is defined in [CARs 700.01](/glossary#acclimatized) as a flight
crew member _"whose biorhythm is aligned with local time"_ — in plain terms, you
sleep at night and are awake during the day **where you are now**.

## Why it matters

The [700.28](/rules/700-28) maximum-FDP tables are read using a start time that
is **the local time at the place you're acclimatized to** — not necessarily the
wall clock where the flight duty period begins (AC 700-047 §§ 4.22–4.26).

- **Acclimatized** to where the FDP begins → start time is just local time.
- **Not acclimatized** → convert your report time to the local time back in the
  zone you're still acclimatized to, and use _that_ row.

Because your home-zone equivalent is often earlier in the day (and the early rows
are the most restrictive), being unacclimatized usually **shortens** your maximum
FDP.

## When you count as acclimatized

An operator may assume you're acclimatized to your **home-base** time zone unless
previous **duty** has acclimatized you elsewhere. Only operating or positioning to
a new zone — and then spending enough time there — changes it; a there-and-back
trip the next day does not (AC 700-047 § 4.23).

After you leave the zone you're acclimatized to, you become acclimatized to the
new one once required rest has been given **and** you've spent **72 hours** there
(time-zone difference under 4 hours) or **96 hours** (difference of 4 hours or
more). Alternatively, your acclimatized time drifts **1 hour toward the new zone
for every 24 hours** you spend there (CARs 700.28(5); AC 700-047 §§ 4.24–4.26).

## A note on other operations

The **medevac** rules (CARs 700.100 series) and **702** aerial-work rules don't
use acclimatization the way 703/704/705 do — check those subparts directly if
that's your operation.
