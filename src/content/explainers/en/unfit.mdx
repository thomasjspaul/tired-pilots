## What the CARs require

[CARs 700.26(1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/section-700.26.html): _"An air operator shall not allow a flight crew member to begin a flight duty period if, before the beginning of the period, the member advises the air operator that they are fatigued to the extent that they are not fit for duty."_

And [700.26(2)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-96-433/section-700.26.html): if you become unfit **during** an FDP, you must tell every other flight crew member and the operator as soon as you're aware of it.

**Fit for duty** is defined in [CARs 101.01](/glossary#fit-for-duty) — your ability to act as a flight crew member is not impaired by fatigue, alcohol or drugs, or any mental or physical condition. Only the flight crew member can decide whether they are fatigued to that extent (AC 700-047 § 4.2).

## The gap: unfit for _this_ duty, fit for a shorter one

The regulation speaks in absolutes — fit or not fit — and says nothing about a crew member who couldn't safely do the assigned long day but could do a short local flight.

AC 700-047 § 4.11(3) fills that gap with guidance, not a rule:

> If a flight crew member were to advise the air operator that they are unfit for the assigned duty but they are fit for a different duty, it would be reasonable for the air operator to reassign the flight crew member to the duty they are fit for.

So: tell the operator you're not fit for the assigned duty (that part is required). Whether you're then reassigned to something shorter is a reasonable operator decision the AC supports — it isn't a right the CARs spell out.
